## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored above the fold, generic below it.**

**LLM assessment.** This is not category-interchangeable at the marketing layer, and that is rare enough to say plainly. `Explorer`, `CanteenHome`, `NetbookSplash`, `About`, `AppCard` and `Loading` deliver the Excavated Console with conviction — blackletter over monospace, dashed accent tape, zero radius, the hard letterpress shadow. Strip the palette and it stays recognizable.

The world collapses at the Operate boundary, and the collapse is traceable to specific files. `RecipeForm.jsx` is 657 lines with zero dashed borders, zero blackletter, one bare chevron, and four blurred `shadow-xl` popovers. `RecipeDetail.jsx:131` — the most-read heading in the product — is `font-mono text-3xl` while every sibling page title is `font-gothic text-4xl`. `Settings.jsx` and `UserList.jsx` are plain tables with `bg-white/5` headers. `Conversation.jsx` is the only Canteen page that doesn't use `MiddenCard` at all. The pattern is consistent: the further a surface is from the front door, the less it believes in the design language.

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs` returned **0 findings** across all five source trees, and the agent verified detector sensitivity with a planted probe file (which correctly fired `gradient-text` and `ai-color-palette`), so the clean result is real rather than a silent failure.

**But the clean scan is not evidence that the palette is disciplined, and the report drawing that inference is wrong.** The `design-system-color` rule matches CSS hex literals; every color in these three apps is a Tailwind utility class, so the rule is structurally blind to them. Direct grep finds **21 raw-palette occurrences in non-test source across all three apps** — `red-200/400/500/600/800/900/950`, `blue-500/900/200`, `gray-400/600` — none of which any token defines. The detector's blind spot is precisely where this codebase's drift lives.

**Visual overlays: none** — no detector overlay was injected. **The open placeholder question is now resolved, and Assessment B's hypothesis was wrong.** `color-scheme` is indeed undeclared (`normal`), but Tailwind v4 Preflight overrides the UA placeholder with `color-mix(in oklab, currentColor 50%, transparent)`. Measured in-page, that composites to `rgb(107,112,121)` on the input fill = **4.0:1** — a real AA failure (needs 4.5:1) affecting every placeholder in the suite, but nowhere near the ~1.0:1 "most severe defect" B projected.

## Overall Impression

The design language is real, and the architecture that carries it across three apps is better than most teams manage. Six token overrides per theme, and all three accents independently clear AA on their own ground — that is a designed constraint, not luck.

What's missing is a *system*, as distinct from a *style*. `DESIGN.md` specifies color, type, radius, and shadow beautifully, and specifies nothing about error surfaces, destructive actions, pagination, empty states, or loading. Those five things then got invented independently in each app — six reds, four confirm patterns, two paginations, three empty-state treatments, five loading treatments. The inconsistency the user suspected is not sloppiness at the edges; it is the exact negative space of the style guide.

**The single biggest opportunity:** every high-severity finding in this report lives in a shared component or belongs in one. Fixing them is centralization, not a rewrite.

## What's Working

**The `[data-theme]` re-tint architecture** (`index.css:37-72`). Six token overrides per theme and nothing else, so every shared component restyles from terracotta to brass with no per-app CSS. The proof it was designed: all three accents clear AA text contrast on their own ground (Canteen 7.81:1, Midden 5.94:1, Netbook 5.49:1). Retheming this way usually breaks contrast in at least one variant.

**`Loading.jsx`.** The aesthetic thesis and the engineering are the same decision, three times: 160ms stepping is discrete because moon phases are discrete; `aria-hidden` on the glyph with `role="status"` on the label preserves the state without the visual; and `prefers-reduced-motion` rests on the full moon rather than hiding, so reduced motion loses the animation but not the information. That last part is what almost nobody gets right.

**The focus-visible shim** (`index.css:95-113`). There are 34 `focus:outline-none` declarations in this codebase. One unlayered `:where(…):focus-visible` rule neutralizes every one of them — winning on cascade layer rather than `!important`, staying keyboard-only, and holding specificity at 0 so per-element overrides still work. The ring measures 13.7–15.3:1 across themes. Note that `DESIGN.md` still lists this as an outstanding defect in two places; **the document is stale, the code is correct.**

**Honorable mention:** `AppCard.jsx:44-78` splits the mobile row into a description-expand button and a separately-labeled navigate target, solving "read the description without leaving" instead of making the whole card tappable and hoping.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] `/login` is half-Midden, half-host-app — and unlabelled

**Corrected after author input.** The original finding called the Midden palette on Canteen's `/login` a bug. It is not: the login screen is a shared identity surface (one `iam-service` account across all three apps) and is **intended** to carry Midden branding regardless of which app the user came from, in the manner of a central SSO page. The heuristic score for Consistency was not changed, because the residual defect below is real.

**What (browser-verified, 1700×1050 and 400×1000):** on `http://localhost:5174/login`, `document.body.dataset.theme` is **absent**, so tokens resolve to Midden's (`--color-dark: #060a08`, `--color-accent: #4a9d5b`, `--color-primary: #210130`) — correct, per intent. But `document.body`'s own background is `rgb(42, 27, 21)` = `#2a1b15`, Canteen's Roast Umber, hardcoded in `Canteen/index.html:12`. The Midden-branded panel therefore sits on the *host app's* ground. `Login.jsx:90`'s `bg-dark min-h-screen` wrapper hides this in the steady state, so it surfaces as a flash of the wrong ground before React mounts and on overscroll rubber-banding — not as a constant mismatch.

The larger gap is that the page **never says "Midden."** Its only heading is the mode label ("Log In" / "2-Factor Verification"). A Canteen user redirected to a green-and-violet screen has nothing telling them this is the shared Midden account system rather than a mis-skinned page — which is exactly the ambiguity the unified branding is meant to remove.

**Fix:** Set `data-theme="midden"` explicitly on the login route rather than relying on the absence of an attribute, so the intent is declared rather than incidental and the ground is Midden's too. Add a Midden wordmark above the mode heading (`font-gothic`, hard shadow), plus a line of the order "One Midden account for Canteen, Netbook, and Midden." Neutralize the per-app `index.html` body background on this route.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] A down API is presented to the user as "you have no data"

**What:** Nine list surfaces branch only on `loading` and `length === 0` (`RecipeList.jsx:14`, `ListList.jsx:12`, `CanteenUserList.jsx:33`, `NoteList.jsx:44`, `Messages.jsx:89`, `UserList.jsx:42`). TanStack's default `retry: 3` with backoff means ~7 seconds of pulsing "Loading recipes…" resolves to **"No recipes found in the canteen."** Only three pages read `isError`, and those render "Recipe not found." / "User not found." — telling a user their own recipe was deleted when the server merely 500'd. There is **no `ErrorBoundary` anywhere in the repo**, so a render throw is a white screen. Six mutations handle failure with nothing but `console.error` — the button visibly does nothing.

**Why it matters:** "You have nothing" and "we're broken" demand opposite responses from a user. Telling a cook their collection is empty is worse than an error; it is a lie that invites them to leave.

**Fix:** Add an `isError` branch to `RecipeList`, `ListList`, `CanteenUserList`, `NoteList` rendering a dashed banner plus a **Retry** wired to `refetch()`. Reserve "not found" for `error.response?.status === 404`. Wrap each `<Outlet />` in `Dashboard.jsx` in one `ErrorBoundary`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] Destructive confirmation has four implementations, and the confirm button fails contrast

**What:** `bg-red-500` + `text-white` computes to **3.76:1** against the 4.5:1 minimum, on the confirm button in `RecipeDetail.jsx:314`, `MyLists.jsx:148`, `Notes.jsx:244`, `CanteenUserList.jsx:133`. It darkens to `red-600` on hover (4.83:1) — so it passes only while the pointer is on it. `RecipeDetail.jsx:211` uses a *different* red for the button that opens that same modal. `ListList.jsx:38` and `Note.jsx:65` use a bare `✕` with `hover:text-red-400`.

And `UserList.jsx:102` uses **`window.confirm()`** — native OS chrome, no theme, no glyph font — in the one flow that permanently destroys another person's account. The most dangerous action in the suite has the least designed confirmation.

Copy diverges too: Midden's native dialog names its target (`delete ${user.username}`), `CanteenUserList` names its target, but `RecipeDetail`, `MyLists` and `Notes` say "this recipe" / "this list" / "this note" — the user cannot verify what they are about to destroy.

**Why it matters:** Delete is the one button where hesitation is a feature. Four visual languages prevent users building a reflex, and the least legible element on screen is the irreversible one.

**Fix:** Promote a `ConfirmModal` into `shared/ui/components/` wrapping `MiddenModal`, taking `{ title, objectName, confirmLabel, onConfirm, isPending }`. Render the destructive action as a dashed red-bordered slab with `text-red-200` (10.4:1 on ground) — in-world *and* accessible. Replace the `window.confirm`. Interpolate the object name into every message.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The theming system has hardcoded leaks — including its own signature effect

**What:** This one neither assessment caught, and it undercuts the system's best asset. The four `@utility text-shadow-hard-*` blocks (`index.css:79-93`) are defined with **literal hex, not tokens**:

```css
@utility text-shadow-hard-grey { text-shadow: 2px 2px 0 #7d7e75; }
```

`#7d7e75` is Midden's grey. Canteen's is `#9c9482` (warm), Netbook's is `#7a8194` (cool). So the signature letterpress shadow — the design system's stated depth device — is theme-blind at **19 usages across 11 files**, including the header wordmark, the moon loader, and every display heading in all three apps. In Canteen a cold grey-green shadow sits behind blackletter on warm terracotta.

Two more leaks in the same family: `MobileBurgerMenu.jsx:33` fills the full-screen mobile nav with `bg-black` rather than `bg-dark`, so mobile navigation is pure `#000` in all three apps instead of each theme's ground; and the 21 off-token `red`/`blue`/`gray` classes noted above are, by definition, identical in every theme.

Separately, `MiddenModal.jsx:8` carries `shadow-xl` — a blurred drop shadow — in the *shared* component, alongside ten more in Canteen's popovers. `DESIGN.md` states "If a shadow has blur, it does not belong to this system." The exemplar breaks its own rule and exports the break to every app.

**Why it matters:** The one-token-swap architecture is the reason three apps can share a UI layer. Every hardcoded value is a place where adding a fourth app, or retinting an existing one, silently produces a wrong result — and these leaks are concentrated in exactly the details that carry the brand.

**Fix:** Redefine the shadow utilities against `var(--color-grey)` etc. so they re-tint. Swap `bg-black` → `bg-dark`. Strip `shadow-xl`/`shadow-lg` from all 11 sites. Add `--color-danger` / `--color-danger-text` tokens per theme and replace the 21 raw classes.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable extract`

### [P2] Two paginations, three icon vocabularies, and a form that loses twenty minutes of work

**What:** Pagination exists twice and neither is the system: `PaginationControls.jsx` (Canteen) has a page-size `Select`, `font-icons` `D`/`B` chevrons, "Page 3" with no total, and infers `isNextDisabled` from a short page. `Notes.jsx:204-224` (Netbook) has no page-size control, Unicode `‹`/`›`, "Page 3 of 7", and a real `totalPages`. Netbook's is more informative; Canteen's is more in-world. The shared component lives in `apps/Canteen/src/components/`, not `shared/`.

Iconography exists three times: the `Midden Icons` font, raw Unicode (21 occurrences — `✕`×5, `•`×11, `‹›`, `→`), and inline `<svg>` checkmarks at `RecipeForm.jsx:483`. `DESIGN.md` says use the glyph font "for all iconography," and close/delete — among the most-used controls in the suite — is precisely the one that never does. `[` is bound to two meanings: burger and drag handle.

And `RecipeForm.jsx` has no unsaved-changes guard — `onCancel` → `navigate(-1)` discards everything silently — while Netbook's far smaller `NoteForm.jsx` persists every keystroke to localStorage. **Netbook protects a two-field note; Canteen does not protect a forty-field recipe.**

**Fix:** Move `PaginationControls` to `shared/ui/components/`, add an optional `totalPages` prop, adopt in `Notes.jsx`. Add close/expand/heart/arrow glyphs to `MiddenIcons.otf` and replace the Unicode. Give the drag handle its own glyph. Reuse the `noteDrafts.js` pattern for recipe drafts, or at minimum confirm-on-dirty-cancel.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable extract`

## Questions to Consider

1. `DESIGN.md` defines no red, no error surface, no empty state, and no pagination — and those four then got invented six, nine, three, and two times. Is the document describing the system or advertising it? What belongs in it that isn't there because it wasn't pretty enough to write down?
2. The moon loader is the suite's one authored motion moment, and it fires only on route transitions — the moments users care least about. What would it mean to spend that craft on the moments that actually hurt: the 2FA wait, the offline flush, the recipe save?
3. Netbook already knows how to hold a user's work when the network dies. Canteen, where someone may have spent twenty minutes on a forty-field recipe, knows none of it. Why did the offline story get built for the cheapest-to-lose content and not the most expensive?
4. Every empty state is a dead end: one sentence, centered. But the premise is a *dig site* — a place where the absence of a find is itself information. What would an empty state look like as an empty excavation grid rather than an apology?
5. `Header.jsx` shows the settings gear only in Midden. Is Midden a portal you *leave*, or a shell the sub-apps *live inside*? The product hasn't decided, which is why account settings are unreachable from two of three apps.
6. The glyph font is described as a point-and-click verb list. Adventure games *show you the verb list*. What if the Midden Icons vocabulary were exposed once — a legend, a hover label — so `uJ` and `Z` became learnable rather than decorative?
